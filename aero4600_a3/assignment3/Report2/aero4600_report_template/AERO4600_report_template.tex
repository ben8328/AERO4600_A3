\section*{Abstract}
This project focuses on the design and implementation of an autopilot system for the longitudinal dynamics of an 
aircraft, with the goal of achieving control and stability over vertical speed and pitch rate. The primary objectives 
include developing a vertical speed guidance loop, integrated with an inner pitch rate control loop, and an auto-throttle 
system to regulate airspeed. Through theoretical analysis and simulation, the system’s performance was evaluated based on 
key criteria, including response time, stability, and robustness to external disturbances. A 6DOF nonlinear simulation was 
used to validate the control laws, demonstrating the autopilot’s ability to track vertical speed and airspeed commands with 
minimal overshoot and fast settling times. The system’s response to gust inputs was also assessed to evaluate its 
disturbance rejection capabilities. The results confirm that the proposed longitudinal autopilot design meets the specified 
performance requirements, providing a reliable solution for maintaining desired flight paths and stability in the longitudinal plane.

\section{Introduction}
Automatic Flight Control Systems (AFCS) are the key system used in modern aviation, providing stability and control to aircraft, while 
reducing pilot workload and increasing overall flight safety. AFCS typically incorporate multi-loop control architectures to manage the various 
degrees of freedom involved in the lateral and longitudinal movements of the aircraft's dynamics. The longitudinal control of an aircraft, which 
focuses on controlling vertical speed and pitch angle, is used for flight phases such as climb, descent, and level flight.

The foundation of AFCS design relies on control theory principles. According to \cite{stevens2016aircraft}, AFCS are structured around feedback 
control loops that use sensor data to continuously monitor and adjust aircraft dynamics to ensure the aircrafts stability during flight command 
inputs. These control loops are constructed to specific flight parameters, such as pitch rate and vertical speed, by typically using 
Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) control, to achieve the desired response. In recent years, advancements in control theory and computational 
power have further advanced AFCS that can handlle multi-input, multi-output (MIMO) control problems, and aids in managing the interactions between 
control surfaces and actuators. 

This study aims to design and implement an autopilot system specifically for the longitudinal dynamics of an aircraft. The design process includes 
the development of a vertical speed guidance loop, integrated with an inner pitch rate controller, and an auto-throttle control loop for airspeed 
management. The vertial speed loop utilizes a componsator to conver vertial speed error into a pitch rate command, while the inner pitch rate 
controller provides finer pitch adjustments. The auto-throttle loop requires the system to regulate airspeed while maintaining the desired vertical 
speed and would need an analysis of the cross-coupling effects of the system.

This study utilizes MATLAB software, specifically using  System Identification Toolbox \cite{MATLAB-SYS-ID}, Control System Toolbox 
\cite{MATLAB-CONTROL}, and Simulink Toolbox \cite{MATLAB-SIMULINK}, to model, design, and simulate the autopilot system. These tools facilitate the 
identification of system dynamics and the development of control strategies based on theoretical models, which are then validated through time-domain 
simulations. As discussed in the course materials by Boyang \citet{lectures}, MATLAB’s System Identification Toolbox is instrumental in deriving 
accurate models for aircraft dynamics, while the Control System Toolbox aids in designing feedback controllers that meet stability and performance 
requirements outlined by the scope of this project.

\section{Q3 -- Vertical Speed Guidance Loop}
The vertical speed guidance loop is designed to control the aircraft’s ascent and descent rates 
by incorporating a pitch rate controller as an inner loop. This loop includes a compensator in 
the forward leg, which processes the vertical speed error to generate a pitch rate command. 
The pitch rate command then feeds into the inner loop to modulate the elevator input, allowing 
the system to adjust vertical speed in response to deviations from the desired reference.

\subsection{Loop Topology}
\label{Loop_Topology}
The complete loop topology of the vertical speed guidance loop is shown in Figure \ref{fig:q3-loop-top}. The
compensator transforms the vertical speed error ($vs_{ref} - vs$) into a pitch rate command. This command is 
then processed within the inner loop section ($de2q$), where the transfer functions $G_1$ and $G_2$ are used in 
the pitch rate controller to compute the required elevator deflection ($\delta_e$) to achieve the target pitch rate.
